# Part 5 — Failure analysis

Format for every case: **Detection → Safe state → Mechanical backup → Software response → Recovery procedure.**
The architecture is Option C (Part 3) with the interlock system of Part 6. "Pin" means the spring-applied, solenoid-released carrier lock; "screw" means the self-locking ball/lead screw of the transformation actuator; "IMD" is the isolation monitoring device (Part 11).

Design principle used throughout: every corner has **three independent means of holding geometry** (pin, self-locking screw, actuator brake) and **two independent means of knowing geometry** (carrier absolute sensor and actuator internal position, cross-checked against the arm sensor). Loss of any one never removes the ability to hold or to know.

| 1 | **Actuator loses power** (one corner, or 24 V bus) | actuator CAN heartbeat lost; 24 V rail monitor; commanded-vs-measured carrier angle diverges | geometry frozen where it is: the screw is non-backdrivable and the brake is spring-applied on power loss | pin drops into the nearest 5° slot as soon as the solenoid is unpowered (it is unpowered whenever the actuator is not commanded) | if the corner is at a settled position: keep mode, log fault, cap speed at 25 km/h if not all corners are at ROAD angle. If mid-move: declare TRANSITION-FAULT, cap 5 km/h, prompt rider to stop; refuse further transformations | dealer/rider: restore 24 V (fuse, connector); controller re-homes by reading absolute sensors, no calibration needed. ## 5.1 Failures that this architecture removes by design

- **Actuator backdrive under impact** (the classic wheel-leg failure): removed because the pin and screw, not the actuator motor, carry driving loads.
- **Steering change during height change**: removed by the tie-rod-on-axis geometry (Part 3.5).
- **Half-shaft over-angle** during geometry change: removed because the motor rides on the carrier.
- **Ski/track "half-deployed" states**: removed because the kits are swap-in and their state is binary (adapter seated or not), verified by a switch and a kit ID.
- **Sinking on wheel retraction failure** (marine variant): removed because the hull is sealed independently of the wheel bays (free-flooding arches, sealed tub).

## 5.2 Failures that remain the biggest residual risks

1. Pivot seal failure on the marine variant (water into the carrier housing where the motor lives). Mitigation: motor is IP67 in its own right; carrier drain; IMD.
2. Lock sector wear from repeated engagements under residual load. Mitigation: unload the pin with the actuator before release (Part 4.4 step 2); hardened sector insert.
3. Controller declaring a mode the hardware has not reached. Mitigation: mode declarations require hardware switch states, not just sensor readings (Part 6).
